You're probably here because something in your product isn't converting the way it should, and you're not sure why. Maybe it's the funnel. Maybe it's onboarding. Maybe it's an integration you haven't built yet, or one you built for the wrong reason. I spent 8+ years as a B2B SaaS PM figuring exactly that out: most recently a year at CloudTalk as Senior Integrations PM, before that at Productboard across Growth and Integrations. Now I'm independent. I advise seed and Series A startups on PLG and product strategy, build my own tools, and run a YouTube channel that has nothing to do with any of this. When something's broken and nobody's sure why, that's usually when I get the call.

## My Experience

I didn't come up the classic PM ladder. I moved into product from operator roles. Most of my career has happened closest to the mess: defining metrics from scratch, running my own user research, sitting in on enterprise negotiations, shipping without waiting for someone to hand me a tidy process. Give me an ambiguous problem and I'd rather go find the real answer than sound confident about a guess. If you'd rather have someone dig into the actual data with you than get handed a 40-slide framework, we'll get along fine.

## Productboard

At Productboard, trial conversion had fallen from being among the best in class to the lowest we'd seen. I ran my own research with twelve ICP buyers, no fancy tooling, and found the real issue was scattered across onboarding, UX, and a value prop that had never been clearly spelled out anywhere. Fixed it myself. Activation improved meaningfully quarter over quarter. Separately, I pushed to build an Azure DevOps integration instead of doubling down on Jira. Everyone wanted the safe move. I didn't take it, and it paid off in accounts and revenue within the year.

## CloudTalk
At CloudTalk, I scaled the integration portfolio from roughly 70 to 100+ live integrations in six months. The number matters less than the framework behind which integrations we actually built: usage data, ICP fit, whether an integration was quietly blocking or winning sales deals, partnership value, and whether it opened a market we weren't even in yet.

## What I'm into
I work with seed and Series A startups — funded teams and solo founders alike — on the questions that come up right when self-serve starts to matter and nobody in the building has fully figured it out yet:

- <b>"Does PLG even make sense for us?"</b> — Not every product should be self-serve. I'll tell you honestly, not just sell you a funnel.
- <b>"Why isn't our self-service funnel converting?"</b> — And more importantly, where the real problem actually lives. Sometimes it's a funnel step. Just as often it's the onboarding, a design choice that's quietly confusing people, or the fact that nobody's ever clearly explained why your product beats the spreadsheet or the workaround they're using now. I don't assume — I go find out.
- <b>"What's our integration and ecosystem strategy?" </b> — Which integrations to build or improve, in what order, and why. This is the area I've gone deepest on, but I treat it as one lever among several, not the whole strategy.
- <b>"How do we prioritize any of this?"</b> — I bring a structured way to weigh market signal, technical risk, and team reality against each other, instead of whoever's loudest in the room winning.

## How we can work together:

1. **One-off calls.** You've got a specific, gnarly problem, we spend an hour (or a few) taking it apart
2. **PLG / product audits.** I go through your funnel, onboarding, and product experience like I would my own team's, and hand you a prioritized list of what's actually broken and why
3. **Ongoing advisory.** Embedded enough to be useful, not so embedded I'm just another headcount



## What makes me different

I don't default to one favorite lever. I've sat on both the growth side and the ecosystem side of the same product, and I've watched teams blame the funnel when the real problem was onboarding messaging, more than once. Checking before diagnosing sounds obvious. It happens less than you'd think.

I don't trust secondhand user knowledge, mine or yours. If I don't know the answer, I go find it: talk to your users, dig through your data, build a scrappy test. And I'm building my own products right now, early and imperfect as they are, which keeps me honest about how hard this actually is when there's no team, no infra, and nobody else to blame when it doesn't work.
