## Fixing a self-serve business that had quietly stopped converting

**Productboard, Growth team**

Trial conversion had fallen from being among the best in class to the lowest we'd seen. Rather than guess at the cause, I ran my own research — twelve ICP buyers, no fancy tooling, just direct conversations and observation.

The real issue turned out to be scattered across three places at once: onboarding assumed context new users didn't have, a couple of UX choices were quietly confusing first-time users, and — most importantly — the actual value prop had never been clearly spelled out anywhere in the product. Users couldn't answer a basic question: why is this better than what I'm already using?

I fixed the UX issues, rewrote the onboarding and trial messaging myself, and worked with design and engineering to rebuild the first-run experience. Activation improved meaningfully, quarter over quarter, and the pattern held.

Separately, on the integrations side of the same team, I made the contrarian call to build an Azure DevOps integration instead of doubling down on Jira, the "safe" obvious move everyone expected. It paid off in accounts and revenue within the year, and set a technical pattern the team reused going forward.

**The takeaway:** a conversion problem is rarely just a funnel problem. Onboarding, UX, and messaging all quietly compound, and you won't find the real cause without going and looking directly at how new users actually experience the product.

## Building the framework behind a fast-scaling integration portfolio

**CloudTalk, Senior Integrations PM**

I scaled the integration portfolio from roughly 70 to 100+ live integrations in six months. The number itself isn't the interesting part — the framework behind *which* integrations we actually built is:

- **Usage data** — what were customers actually asking for or already using workarounds for
- **ICP fit** — did this integration serve the customers we were actually trying to win
- **Sales conversion signal** — was the lack of an integration quietly blocking deals, or was an existing one winning them
- **Partnership strategic value** — did this open a relationship worth more than the integration itself
- **New market value** — could this integration open up a market we weren't even in yet

That framework is the same lens I now bring to any "which integrations should we build" conversation — it's rarely just about volume, it's about ranking the right things for the right reasons.

**The takeaway:** speed without a framework is just noise. Scaling fast only works if you know *why* each thing you're building is worth building before you start.
